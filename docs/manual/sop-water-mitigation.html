Chapter 1

How this SOP works

Everyone reads this chapter

Every water job we run ends with a carrier deciding whether to pay for it. The adjuster was not there. All they will ever see is what we wrote down: the photos, the moisture map, the drying log and the Certificate of Drying. This SOP exists so that every tech documents a job the same way, in the same order, with numbers that can be repeated by anyone who walks in after us.

Each step names the screen in the field app where it gets recorded. If a step is done but not in the app, for billing purposes it did not happen.

The job from start to finish

Who does what

RoleOwns
Tech (crew)Takes every reading, photo and equipment entry in the app, on site, at the time. Equipment goes in by scanning each unit's label. Follows the protocol exactly and calls the crew lead the moment something on the escalation list shows up.
Crew lead (CJ)Sets the category, class and reference points on day 0 when on site, checks each day's readings before the crew leaves, verifies dry before equipment is pulled, and prepares the Certificate of Drying.
Branden (IICRC WRT)Approves any departure from the equipment worksheet, any Cat 3 or asbestos decision and any change of meter mid-job. Signs the Certificate of Drying as the certified technician.

The six rules that never bend

  1. Photos before you touch anything. The first picture of a room shows it the way the customer found it.
  2. Same meter, same setting, same spot, same order, every day. A reading that can't be repeated is worthless to an adjuster.
  3. Reference first. Every material gets an unaffected reference reading on every visit, and its dry goal comes from that reference.
  4. Record it at the point. Readings go into the app where you are standing, not from memory in the truck.
  5. Every 24 hours. A job with no reading in 36 hours shows a red flag on the job list. Don't let it.
  6. Nothing gets unplugged until it's verified dry, and nothing gets cut, torn out or bagged without a photo and a reason written down.
Chapter 2

Before you roll

Every tech, every morning

Truck kit

Meters and documentation

  • Pin meter with insulated pins (wood and drywall, WME scale)
  • Non-penetrating meter (search / REL scale)
  • Thermo-hygrometer (temperature and RH)
  • Thermal camera, if assigned (it finds suspects; it never replaces a meter)
  • Calibration check block or the meter's built-in check
  • Spare batteries for every meter
  • Blue painter's tape and a fine Sharpie for marking points
  • Phone charged, Field Forms opened once on Wi-Fi this morning

Safety and work gear

  • Nitrile gloves, N95s, safety glasses; Tyvek and P100 for Cat 3
  • GFCI cords; know the panel before anything plugs in
  • Moisture-proof flashlight and a non-contact voltage tester
  • Extraction gear, 6-mil poly, zip poles, antimicrobial
  • Drill and bits for weep holes (only with an OK)
  • A readable QR asset label on every dehu, air mover, scrubber and heater
  • Winter: ice cleats, a heater, and a warm spot for the meters
Asset tagsEach unit's label shows its tag in large print with a QR code that carries the same tag. Tags look like AM-014: two letters for the kind of unit (AM air mover, DH dehumidifier, AF air scrubber, HT heater) and the unit's number. The fleet list in Office Admin → Equipment is the master list and the labels print from it, so a tag is never made up or hand-written on site. A unit with a missing or torn label: tell the office so it prints a new one.

Meter check

Do this before the first job of the day, at room temperature, not in the truck.

  1. Fresh battery check on each meter. A low battery reads wrong before it reads nothing.
  2. Run the calibration check (test block or the meter's built-in check) and confirm it reads in range. If it fails, don't use that meter today; tell CJ.
  3. Look at the pins: bent, dull or missing insulation sleeves change the reading.
  4. On the first drying-log row of the day, write Cal OK in Notes. That one line answers the adjuster's first question about every number on the page.
Cold meters lieA meter or hygrometer that rode in a cold truck reads low and slow until it warms up. Carry them in the cab, and give a hygrometer 10 to 15 minutes inside the house before you trust it. See chapter 7.
Chapter 3

Day 0: the first visit

Tech and crew lead; follow the steps in order

Arrive and make it safe

1

Walk the outside, then the inside, before you unload

Look for live electrical in or near water, sagging ceilings, a gas smell, sewage, and anyone in the home who is vulnerable (infant, elderly, ill). If power has to come off, it comes off at the panel before anyone walks in standing water.

2

Confirm the source is stopped

Shut-off closed, line capped, or the leak otherwise stopped. If it isn't, stopping it is the first job. Write the cause in plain words; you will type it into Loss Cause in step 5.

3

Check the house's age before anything gets cut

Built before 1981: drywall mud, textured ceilings, plaster and some flooring can contain asbestos. Built before 1978: paint can contain lead. Don't cut, sand or tear out until Branden has cleared it (testing, or work under his EPA RRP certification). Drying can start; demolition waits.

Authorization and the job header

4

Get the Work Authorization signed

Open the job, then ✍️ Work Authorization. Walk the owner through the scope list, check the boxes, and have them sign on the phone with Sign on device. Sign as the Roybal representative. Emergency stop-the-source work can happen first; nothing else does.

The owner must be the property owner or someone they've authorized in writing. A tenant can't authorize work on the structure; call Branden.

5

Take before photos, then fill in the job header

In 📷 Job Photos, shoot every affected room from the doorway, then the source, then close-ups of damage, before you move anything. Stage: Before. Every photo needs a room and a caption.

Then tap the job's edit button and fill in Customer, Address, Claim #, Date of Loss, Carrier, Adjuster and Loss Cause. These flow into every form, so they're typed once.

Category and class

6

Set the category from the source and the time

Category is about what's in the water. Under Loss classification, pick Water, then the category. When in doubt, go up a category, never down, and tell CJ.

CategoryWhat it looks likeWhat changes for us
Cat 1 CleanSupply line, faucet, water heater tank, ice melt that hasn't touched dirty materials; under 48 hours.Dry in place where possible. Pad and drywall can often be saved.
Cat 2 GrayDishwasher or washer discharge, aquarium, toilet overflow with urine only, Cat 1 left more than 48 hours.Antimicrobial on affected surfaces. Carpet pad is removed. Air scrubber when the worksheet calls for one, or a written reason it isn't needed.
Cat 3 BlackSewage, toilet with feces, ground or flood water, anything that sat long enough to smell, Cat 2 left more than 48 hours.PPE, containment and HEPA air scrubbers. Porous materials it touched are removed and bagged. Call Branden before demo.
7

Set the class from how much got wet

Class drives the dehumidifier math. Pick it after your day 0 moisture scan (step 9), and change it on the edit screen if the scan shows more than you thought.

ClassWhat got wet
Class 1Part of one room, mostly low-porosity materials (plywood, concrete). Little water absorbed.
Class 2A whole room or more; carpet and pad; water wicked up walls 12 to 24 in.
Class 3Water came from above: ceilings, insulation, walls wet more than 24 in. up, carpet and subfloor saturated.
Class 4Deep-held water in dense materials: hardwood, plaster, concrete, crawlspace soil. Needs low-grain or desiccant dehus and more time.

Also set the Drying System: Closed (doors and windows shut, dehus do the work) is our default in Fairbanks for most of the year. Open or Hybrid uses outside air and needs Branden's OK; see chapter 7.

Map the wet area

8

Take the reference readings

Before any affected reading, find an unaffected spot of each material that got wet and read it. Chapter 4 covers how. Write it down; it becomes the dry goal.

9

Find the wet boundary and draw the map

Start at the source and scan outward with the non-penetrating meter until the readings match the reference. Then open 🗺️ Moisture Map, import the floor plan (or draw one), and follow chapter 4 to set the material, meter, dry goal and numbered reading points. One map per material.

Extract, remove, treat

10

Extract standing water

Extraction removes far more water than any dehumidifier. Make passes until no water is pulled. Photo during, stage During.

11

Remove what can't be dried, and write down why

Flood cuts, pad, wet insulation, swollen particle board, Cat 3 porous materials. Before anything comes out: a photo of it in place and a reading on it. Flood cuts go at least 12 in. above the highest wet reading, at a standard height (2 ft or 4 ft) so the rebuild is clean. Any reading point that gets cut out keeps its number; note removed day 0 in that day's Notes and leave the column blank after.

On a Cat 3 job, the app won't let the packet clear until the flood cut, containment and HEPA work is documented. Write it as a 🔁 Change Order that names the flood cut, containment, HEPA and antimicrobial work.

12

Clean and apply antimicrobial (Cat 2 and Cat 3)

EPA-registered product, label directions, on the affected structural surfaces after removal. Photo it and add a caption naming the product.

Size and set equipment

13

Run the equipment worksheet before you unload the dehus

Open 💧 Drying Log. Under Equipment Sizing, enter the wet floor SF, affected wall LF, wet wall and ceiling above 2 ft, insets over 18 in., ceiling height and the dehu you have, then tap 🧮 Size the equipment. This is the IICRC WRT worksheet. It gives a recommended range for air movers, dehumidifiers, scrubbers and heat.

14

Set the equipment and scan every unit in

Air movers aim along the wet wall at 15 to 45 degrees to the surface, all turning the same way around the room. Dehus go central, exhaust into the room and away from walls, drain line run to a sink or pump. Then in Equipment Deployment & Runtime tap 📷 Scan equipment, pick the room, check the toggle says Place, and scan each unit's label as it starts running. Each scan adds the unit's row with its asset tag, type, room, and placed date and time. Pick the next room with the room button before you scan that room's units, then tap Done. Check the count at the bottom of the scanner against the units you set. Place the same units on the map's Equipment Placement plan so the adjuster can see the layout.

Type only when a label won't read. Wipe it and try the light first, then use ⌨ Type tag in the scanner; it logs the tag with the same time and room. Only if the scanner can't open at all, add the row by hand with + Add equipment: asset tag, type, room, placed date and time.

If what you set is below the worksheet range, type the reason in the Deviation column (power limits, staged demo, Branden's call). A blank deviation next to a red number is the first line an adjuster cuts.

Before you leave

15

Take the day 0 readings, after the equipment is running

One psychrometric row in the drying log (chapter 5) and one reading row on each moisture map (chapter 4). These are the numbers every later day is compared to.

16

Brief the owner and set the next visit

Tell them: don't turn equipment off, don't open windows, don't move air movers, keep the heat on, and expect us within 24 hours. Leave the office number. If the job has a customer portal, the office turns it on; don't promise dates.

17

Write the day's Field Report

📋 Field Report: what was done, what was found, materials used, anything the office needs to know. It stays internal; it isn't sent to the carrier.

Chapter 4

The moisture reading protocol

The standard every reading on every Roybal job follows

A moisture reading is only useful if the next tech can stand in the same place, with the same meter on the same setting, and get a number you can compare. Everything in this chapter is there to make that true. The protocol has seven parts, and they happen in this order on day 0. From day 1 on, parts 6 and 7 repeat on every visit.

1 · One meter, one setting, per material

  • Pick the meter and setting for each material on day 0 and record it in the map's Meter / Setting box. Use ➕ New meter / setting… the first time; the phone remembers it after that. Write the setting, not just the brand: Protimeter MMS3 · Search (REL) or Protimeter MMS3 · Pin (WME).
  • Drywall and plaster: non-penetrating (search / REL) for mapping, with pins to confirm. Wood, subfloor and framing: pins on the WME or wood scale. Concrete: the meter's concrete setting, or a reading at a drilled hole if Branden calls for it.
  • Never switch meters or settings mid-job. If a meter dies, the next meter starts a new map page: re-read the reference and every point with it, and write meter changed in that row's Notes. Tell Branden.

2 · The reference reading

The reference is the same material, read with the same meter and setting, in a spot the water never reached. It tells us what "dry" is for that material in that house, in that season.

  • Same kind of spot: interior wall for interior walls, exterior wall for exterior walls. In winter an exterior wall reads differently than an interior one.
  • Same building, same floor level, preferably a room on the same heat. A closet on the far side of the house is ideal.
  • Take it first on every visit, before any affected reading, and write it as the first words of that row's Notes: REF 12 (bedroom closet).
  • If the reference moves by more than 2 points from day 0, something changed (season, heat, meter). Note it and tell CJ before using today's numbers to call anything dry.

3 · Setting the dry goal

Roybal rule: dry goal = reference + 10%

Take the reference reading and add 10% of it. Round to the nearest whole number. If that adds less than 1 point, add 1 point.

Reference+10%Dry goal
12 REL (drywall)13.213
11 WME (subfloor)12.112
8 REL (drywall)8.89 (minimum +1)

Type the goal into Dry Goal (MC%). Picking a Material fills in a textbook goal (for example drywall ≤ 1%), which only fits some meters' scales. Our goal comes from the reference, so it always replaces the textbook number.

No unaffected reference anywhere (the whole level got wet)? Use the meter maker's dry value for that setting, write no ref, maker's dry value in Notes, and tell Branden.

4 · Placing reading points

Reading points go where the water was worst, where it stopped, and in between, so the trend shows the whole area drying and not just one easy spot.

SurfaceWhere the points go
WallsOne point every 4 ft (no wider) along each wet wall, 2 in. above the bottom plate or the top of the base. If water wicked more than 12 in., add a high point just under the wet line. Corners and both sides of a wet wall cavity count as separate points.
FloorsA grid no wider than 4 ft across the wet area, plus one at the source, one at each doorway the water crossed, and one at the toe kick of each cabinet run (pull the toe kick).
CeilingsThe center of the stain and each edge. Only after the ceiling is confirmed safe to stand under.
BoundaryAt least one point 12 in. past the wet edge on each map. It should read like the reference. If it climbs, the water is spreading and the map has to grow.
Hidden cavitiesBehind cabinets, tubs and built-ins: read through a weep hole only after CJ or Branden OKs drilling it. Photo the hole.

Each map holds one material and up to 13 points on screen (+ Add locations 14–26 adds more). A room with wet drywall and a wet subfloor gets two maps, because each material has its own meter setting and its own goal.

5 · Numbering and marking

  1. Number clockwise from the source (or from the entry door when the source is in the middle). Boundary points get the last numbers.
  2. On the map, tap ① Number and tap each point's spot on the plan in that order. The app numbers them for you. Draw the day 0 wet boundary with a color.
  3. Mark each point on the wall or floor: a 1-in. square of painter's tape with the number in Sharpie, set just beside the point. Read next to the tape, never through it.
  4. Pin readings go back in the same holes every day.
  5. Numbers are never reused. A point that gets cut out keeps its number with removed day 2 in Notes. A new point gets the next unused number.

6 · Taking a reading

  1. Reference first, written into Notes, then points in number order, one map at a time.
  2. Non-penetrating: the pad flat and fully on the surface, at least 2 in. from screws, corner bead, nail plates and pipes. Metal reads wet.
  3. Pins: same depth every time, in the same holes, held the way the meter maker says for the grain. Do it the same way each day.
  4. Hold until the number settles. If it jumps around, take three readings and record the middle one.
  5. Readings that look wrong (a dry point suddenly at 99) get re-read and photographed with the meter on the spot before you record them.
  6. Same time window every day: within 2 hours of the day 0 time, and never more than 24 hours apart.
  7. Photo the meter on the point for the worst point and the reference on every visit, and for every point on day 0 and on the final day. Caption: Pt 3, 74 REL, day 0.

7 · Logging it in the app

  • On each visit tap + Add reading date (the date fills in) and type each point's value into its numbered column while standing at the point.
  • Notes for the row: the reference first (REF 12), then anything that changed: a point removed, equipment moved, a new point added, a meter change.
  • A blank cell means "not read." Never leave a point blank because it was dry; read it and write the number.
  • The 🎙️ Transcribe button at the top of the form takes spoken readings when there's signal. Read back every value before you confirm it.
  • Paper only if the phone is dead. Then type the paper readings in before you leave the driveway, and photo the paper sheet.

Before you leave the job, open the map's Drying Trend. Every line should be lower than yesterday and heading to the dashed goal line. A line that went flat or went up is the job's problem for today; chapter 6 says what to do about it.

The trend chart prints with the map in the carrier packet. A clean, steady drop on that chart is the best argument for our equipment days.

Chapter 5

Psychrometric readings

Air readings, taken first on every visit

Moisture readings tell us how wet the materials are. Psychrometric readings tell us whether the air is able to pull that water out. Take them first, before you open doors, move equipment or start working, so they show the drying conditions we left overnight.

ReadingWhereDrying-log columns
O OutsideIn shade, about 10 ft from the house, away from dryer and furnace vents.Outside / Ambient T and RH
U UnaffectedA room the water never reached, door shut, same floor if possible.Unaffected (Ref.) T and RH
A AffectedCenter of each affected room, chest high, at least 3 ft out of any air mover's stream and the dehu's exhaust.Affected T and RH (one row per room if there are several, room in Notes)
D Dehu exhaustIn the dehu's exhaust stream.Notes, as Dehu out 84°F / 17%

How to take them

  1. Let the hygrometer settle: at least 2 minutes in each spot, and 10 to 15 minutes after coming in from a cold truck.
  2. Tap + Add reading on the drying log. Date and time, then T and RH for outside, unaffected and affected.
  3. Fill in the equipment counts (Dehu, AM, Scrb) for what is running right now, and your name under Tech.
  4. Put Cal OK (first row of the day) and the dehu exhaust reading in Notes.

Chapter 6

Daily monitoring visits

Every 24 hours until verified dry

The visit, in order

  1. Look. Walk every affected room before touching anything. Is everything running? Any new staining, smell, sag or water? Did the owner move or unplug anything? Photo anything new.
  2. Air. Psychrometric row in the drying log (chapter 5).
  3. Materials. Reference, then every point on every map, in number order (chapter 4).
  4. Equipment. Empty or check the dehu drain, clean filters, re-aim air movers onto the wettest points. Open 📷 Scan equipment: units pulled today get scanned in Remove, which puts the Removed date and time on their row; units added get scanned in Place, which adds their row. A unit moved to another room keeps its row: pick the new room and scan it in Place, and the app adds a Notes line such as moved to Bedroom 2 10/09 14:30. Update the plan on the map if the layout changed.
  5. Photos. Meter on the worst point and on the reference; anything removed or changed today. Stage During.
  6. Trend. Check the Drying Trend on each map and the flags on the job list. Then write the Field Report.
Pull equipment as areas dryWhen every point a unit serves is at goal, that unit comes out that day: scan it out in Remove (that sets its Removed date and time), and add a Notes line such as pulled, area at goal. Running equipment on dry material is the second line an adjuster cuts.

Chapter 7

Fairbanks cold-weather rules

Interior Alaska changes the drying math; these rules apply October through April and any cold night
C1

Frozen water reads dry

Meters read liquid water. Ice in drywall, framing or a crawlspace reads like dry material. On any freeze-up loss, or in a garage, crawlspace or unheated addition, warm the area to above 50°F and hold it for several hours before taking day 0 readings. Check material temperature with the IR thermometer and put it in Notes (material 52°F). Re-read everything after it thaws; the real wet area is often bigger than the frozen one looked.

C2

Keep the heat on, and add heat when the room runs cold

Affected air below 70°F slows drying and cuts LGR capacity. Keep the house thermostat up, and add a heater when the sizing check says so. Scan heaters in like any other unit (HT tags); they show on the certificate's equipment summary.

C3

Closed drying is the default in winter

Fairbanks winter air is extremely dry (often under 10 GPP outside), which is tempting, but opening a house at 20 below freezes pipes and wastes the customer's heat. Open or hybrid drying with outside air only with Branden's OK, and it gets written on the drying log as the Drying System.

C4

Watch combustion appliances

Oil furnaces, boilers, wood and pellet stoves and gas water heaters need their air. Never set an air scrubber to run as negative air in the same space as a fuel-burning appliance without Branden's OK; it can pull exhaust back into the house. A CO alarm goes in any job with containment and a fuel-burning appliance.

C5

Exterior walls and condensation

Exterior wall references read differently from interior ones in winter, so match the reference to the wall type. Frost on window frames, sill plates or rim joists can melt into new water and look like a stalled point. Check for it before calling a point stalled.

C6

Ice dams and snowmelt

Ice-dam water that ran through insulation and the ceiling is at least Cat 2. It can keep coming in on warm afternoons, so read the ceiling points at the same time each day and look outside at the eaves. Tell the office if the roof still has a dam; drying won't finish until it's dealt with.

C7

Cold gear and cold drains

Meters ride in the cab. Dehu drain lines never run outside in winter; drain to a sink, tub or a pump into the house's plumbing. If a visit is missed because of roads or weather, write that in the next Field Report and in the next reading row's Notes so the gap is explained.

Chapter 8

Dry verification and the certificate

Crew lead verifies and prepares; Branden signs

Is it dry?

A job is dry when all of these are true on the same visit:

  1. Every reading point on every map is at or below its dry goal (every cell green).
  2. The reference reads within 2 points of its day 0 value.
  3. Wood, subfloor and framing that were mapped with the non-penetrating meter are confirmed with pins.
  4. No point reached goal for the first time today. If one did, keep the equipment running on it and confirm it at the next visit.
  5. CJ or Branden has looked at the numbers (in person or in the app) and said "dry."

Pulling equipment

  1. Final reading row on every map and a final psychrometric row, Notes Dry verified with who verified it.
  2. Every remaining unit gets scanned out in Remove as it's unplugged, which puts its Removed date and time in the drying log. A unit that won't scan: ⌨ Type tag in the scanner, or type its Removed date and time on its row. No unit is left without a Removed time. Set the Dry-out Finish Date; the banner switches to "Drying complete — N days."
  3. After photos of every affected room from the same doorway spots as the before photos, plus the meter on the worst point at its final reading. Stage After, captioned.
  4. Leave the tape on the points for the rebuild crew and the adjuster, unless the owner asks for it off.
